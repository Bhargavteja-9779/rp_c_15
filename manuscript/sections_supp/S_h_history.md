# Supplementary Note S8. How the method changed during development

Our first idea did not work. We expected that correcting each new lot's bias from a few reference fruit, with the correction shrunk towards past lots and its error calibrated on past lots, would be enough. On the first season we examined it was not: coverage was {{f"{r.season_cov_t('lotcp_shrink',10,2018):.2f}"}} at a nominal 0.90 with ten reference fruit, because lots of the new season differed from past lots in the spread of their errors, not only in their offset. The spread could be estimated in part without any reference value, from the scatter between repeated scans of the same fruit, and adding it repaired coverage. A second component we had designed in, the shrinkage of the bias towards past lots, later failed outright on soil spectra from a new survey campaign and was removed. And when we confirmed the frozen method on a data set we had not used before, the efficiency gain over a simple bias correction did not reach significance, whereas the lot-level guarantee did. The paragraph below summarises these failures, because they define what the method can and cannot claim.

*What did not work first, and why.* Four results from our own work shaped the method and the claims (journal entries in the study repository, available on request). (i) The pre-specified location-only correction under-covered on the first season examined, because the residual scale of new lots exceeded that of past lots; a lot-specific scale was added, partly estimated from repeated scans (protocol deviation D1). (ii) Empirical-Bayes shrinkage of the bias estimate, part of the original design, gave no benefit on mango and failed on the soil survey's campaign shift (coverage {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s_shrink',5):.2f}"}} instead of {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s',5):.2f}"}} for pH at five reference samples); it was removed. (iii) The point predictor we used throughout, PLS after standard normal variate correction, proved {{f"{r.repro('pls_ours','val_ext')-r.repro('pls_published','val_ext'):.2f}"}} % DMC worse in RMSEP than the published PLS recipe on the published split (Section 4); the interval conclusions were therefore re-checked with the published recipe. (iv) In the pre-specified confirmation on a data set not used during development, the lot-level assurance was confirmed but the efficiency gain of LotCP-S over a simple bias correction was not (Section 4).

In a further round after an internal, AI-simulated review (main text, Section 3.8), additional analyses were run under a new internal plan: season-level inference, baselines that share the lot scale of LotCP-S but omit the replay, and decision outcomes with season-cluster confidence intervals at an operating point fixed before the intervals were computed, and leverage-based PLS intervals. They led to the restatement of the efficiency and decision claims in the main text.

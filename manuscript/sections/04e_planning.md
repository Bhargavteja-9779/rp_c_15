# 9. Planning the reference budget before the season

Fig. 13 compares the width planned from the source seasons (Section 4.9) with the width realised on the lots of the target season, for the five target seasons, three predictors, five values of $m$ and both variants.

![Fig. 13. Reference-budget planning. (a) Interval width planned from past lots against the mean width realised on the lots of the following season; each point is one target season, predictor, number of reference samples and variant. (b) Planned and realised budget curves for PLS, averaged over target seasons.](figures/fig13_planning.png){width=6.3in}

The planned width is a usable forecast. Over all {{len(r.plan)}} combinations the median absolute relative difference between realised and planned width is {{f"{100*r.plan.rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} %, and the mean difference is {{f"{100*r.plan.rel_error.mean():.0f}"}} %. For PLS and LightGBM the median absolute difference is {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.model=='pls'].rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} % and {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.model=='gbm'].rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} %; for the CNN it is {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.model=='cnn'].rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} %. By target season the mean difference ranges from {{f"{100*r.plan.groupby('target').rel_error.mean().min():.0f}"}} % to {{f"{100*r.plan.groupby('target').rel_error.mean().max():.0f}"}} %: the forecast is too wide for 2017, when the calibration rested on two source seasons whose season-held-out models were weak, and within {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.target>2017].groupby('target').rel_error.mean().abs().max():.0f}"}} % for the four later seasons (supplementary table S15). The shape of the curve is reproduced (Fig. 13b): most of the reduction in width is obtained with the first five reference samples, and beyond ten both curves flatten. The largest deviations in Fig. 13a are the CNN in 2017, where the planned width is well above the realised one. Table 108 puts the planner in the form a grower would use: for each number of reference fruit, the width of the error bar to expect, the share of lots in which it will hold for at least 90 % of the fruit, and the cost of the tests. Going from no reference fruit to three narrows the LotCP-S interval from {{f"{r.plannerv(0,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} to {{f"{r.plannerv(3,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} % DMC; going from ten to twenty narrows it only from {{f"{r.plannerv(10,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} to {{f"{r.plannerv(20,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} % DMC, at twice the laboratory effort. A grower who needs the error bar to hold within nine lots in ten chooses LotCP-A and accepts a wider interval ({{f"{r.plannerv(10,'planned_width_lotcp_a'):.1f}"}} % DMC at m = 10). Combined with the cost model of Section 8.3, the planner turns the question "how many fruit should we cut?" into a calculation that can be made before the season from the calibration set.

# 14. Lot-level assurance

The marginal method leaves a minority of lots badly covered (Section 11), and near-nominal average coverage means that in a large share of lots the realised coverage is below the nominal level: with LotCP-S and ten reference samples only {{r.meet('lotcp_s',10)}} % of lot-draws reach 0.90. Table 14 and Fig. 12 show what LotCP-A (Section 4.8) achieves when it is asked for 0.90 coverage in at least 90 % of lots.

With $\delta=0.10$, the share of lot-draws that reach 0.90 coverage is {{r.meet('lotcp_a',3)}} %, {{r.meet('lotcp_a',5)}} %, {{r.meet('lotcp_a',10)}} %, {{r.meet('lotcp_a',20)}} % and {{r.meet('lotcp_a',30)}} % for $m=3$, 5, 10, 20 and 30, against a target of 90 %; with $\delta=0.20$ it is {{r.meet('lotcp_a20',5)}} % and {{r.meet('lotcp_a20',10)}} % at $m=5$ and 10, against a target of 80 %. The share of lot-draws with coverage below 0.80 falls from {{r.u('lotcp_s',10)}} % under LotCP-S to {{r.u('lotcp_a',10)}} % at $m=10$. By target season the share reaching 0.90 at $m=10$ ranges over {{r.season_meets('lotcp_a',10)}} % ({{r.season_meets('lotcp_s',10)}} % for LotCP-S), so the assurance, like the marginal coverage, is met on average over seasons and missed by a few points in the less favourable ones. Across the other predictors and the leave-one-season-out protocol the share at $m=10$ is {{f"{100*min(r.gen(a,b,'lotcp_a',10,'meets') for a,b in [('gbm','forward'),('cnn','forward'),('pls','loso'),('cnn','loso')]):.0f}"}}–{{f"{100*max(r.gen(a,b,'lotcp_a',10,'meets') for a,b in [('gbm','forward'),('cnn','forward'),('pls','loso'),('cnn','loso')]):.0f}"}} %. Without reference samples the assurance is not attained ({{r.meet('lotcp_a',0)}} %) and the intervals are very wide ({{r.w('lotcp_a',0)}} % DMC).

The price is width. At $m=10$ the LotCP-A interval is {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_a',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % wider than that of LotCP-S ({{r.w('lotcp_a',10)}} against {{r.w('lotcp_s',10)}} % DMC) and at $m=20$ it is {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_a',20,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',20,'width'))}} % wider. None of the alternatives offers the property. Bias correction with the source quantile reaches 0.90 in {{r.meet('biascorr_naive',10)}} % of lot-draws at $m=10$. Target-only calibration is valid within each lot only on average over reference draws, so the realised coverage of a given draw scatters around the nominal level: the centred version reaches 0.90 in {{r.meet('target_only_centred',10)}} % of lot-draws at $m=10$, where it is conservative and {{r.pct(r.m('target_only_centred',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_a',10,'width'))}} % wider than LotCP-A, and in only {{r.meet('target_only_centred',20)}} % and {{r.meet('target_only_centred',30)}} % at $m=20$ and 30. At twenty reference samples LotCP-A is the only method examined that keeps nine lots in ten at the stated coverage, which is the regime in which the marginal comparison of Section 6.3 found no advantage for LotCP-S over target-only calibration.

On the soil data the share of lots reaching 0.90 at $m=10$ is {{f"{100*r.soil('kssl_oc','lotcp_a',10,'meets'):.0f}"}} % for held-out KSSL projects, where lots are exchangeable, and {{f"{100*r.soil('lucas_oc','lotcp_a',10,'meets'):.0f}"}} % and {{f"{100*r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_a',10,'meets'):.0f}"}} % for LUCAS organic carbon and pH, where the between-campaign change of residual scale again costs a few points; for LotCP-S the corresponding shares are {{f"{100*r.soil('kssl_oc','lotcp_s',10,'meets'):.0f}"}} %, {{f"{100*r.soil('lucas_oc','lotcp_s',10,'meets'):.0f}"}} % and {{f"{100*r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s',10,'meets'):.0f}"}} % (supplementary table S13).

LotCP-S and LotCP-A answer different questions with the same calibration data. The first gives the narrowest interval that is right on average over lots; the second gives an interval that is right for the lot in hand in nine cases out of ten. For a decision that is taken lot by lot, the second statement is the one that corresponds to the risk the grower carries.

# Supplementary Note S5. Ablation, generalisation, soil spectra and failure analysis

## S5.1 Ablation study

Table 6 and Fig. 6 remove or replace one component at a time.

{{T:ablation}}

![Fig. 6. Ablation: lot-macro coverage with bootstrap 95 % CI for m = 5 and m = 20; the interval score (IS) is printed next to each variant.](figures/fig6_ablation.png){width=6.3in}

*Scale.* The lot-specific scale is the component that matters most once $m>0$. Removing it (LotCP) raises the interval score at $m=10$ from {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}} to {{r.i('lotcp',10)}}. The two sources of scale information both contribute: with the reference-sample variance alone the score is {{r.i('lotcp_s_support',10)}}, with the replicate-scan variance alone {{r.i('lotcp_s_rep',10)}}. The replicate-scan term is the only one available at $m\le1$ and costs no reference measurement. It is also what separates LotCP-S from the bias-correction shortcut when reference samples are few. Without the replicate-scan term, the per-lot interval score of the reference-scale-only variant is worse than that of bias correction with the source quantile at $m=3$ (mean difference {{f"{r.test_b('biascorr_naive',3).mean_diff:.2f}"}}, Holm-adjusted p {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',3)}}), because a scale estimated from three samples is noisy, does not differ from it at $m=5$ or 10 (p {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',5)}} and {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',10)}}) and is better only from $m=20$ (p {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',20)}}; supplementary table S6b). A user who takes a single scan per fruit should therefore not expect an efficiency gain over that shortcut with fewer than about twenty reference samples; what remains is the stability of coverage across predictors shown in Supplementary Note S5.

*Fold unit.* At $m=0$, residuals held out by lot or by sample give coverage {{r.c('lotcp_lotfold',0)}} and {{r.c('lotcp_samplefold',0)}}; season-held-out residuals are needed to reach the nominal level. With $m>0$ the picture changes. After the lot bias has been corrected, what is left to calibrate is the within-lot residual, and lot-fold residuals describe it well: LotCP on lot-fold residuals covers {{r.c('lotcp_lotfold',10)}} at $m=10$, slightly below nominal, with narrower intervals than LotCP on season-fold residuals, which over-covers ({{r.c('lotcp',10)}}). LotCP-S on lot-fold residuals covers {{r.c('lotcp_s_lotfold',10)}} with an interval score of {{r.i('lotcp_s_lotfold',10)}}, against {{r.c('lotcp_s',10)}} and {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}} for season-fold residuals. The lot-fold variant of LotCP-S is therefore the more efficient one, and it is not less safe by season: its lowest per-season coverage at $m=10$ is {{r.season_min('lotcp_s_lotfold',10)}}, against {{r.season_min('lotcp_s',10)}} for season-fold residuals. Season-held-out residuals are thus needed without reference samples and are not needed with them. We keep the season-fold variant as the reported method because it was fixed before this comparison was made, and note that the choice costs about {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s_lotfold',10,'width'))}} % in width at $m=10$.

*Shrinkage.* The pre-specified empirical-Bayes shrinkage of the bias estimate has a small effect at $m=3$ (interval score {{r.i('lotcp_shrink',3)}} against {{r.i('lotcp',3)}} for the plain mean with the location-only method, {{r.i('lotcp_s_shrink',3)}} against {{r.i('lotcp_s',3)}} with LotCP-S) and none from $m=10$ ({{r.i('lotcp_s_shrink',10)}} against {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}}). The reason is visible in the variance components: the lot bias is large relative to the sample noise, so the shrinkage weight is already {{r.tau_sigma()['w3']}} at $m=3$. On the soil data of Supplementary Note S5 shrinkage is harmful. The part of H4 that concerns shrinkage is not supported, and the reported method uses the plain mean.

*Episodic replay for LotCP-S.* A pre-specified ablation (plan_v2) removed the replay from LotCP-S, standardising each past lot with its own full-information bias and scale. Coverage then fell to {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s_noepisodic',0,'coverage'):.3f}"}} at m = 0, {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s_noepisodic',3,'coverage'):.3f}"}} at m = 3 and {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s_noepisodic',10,'coverage'):.3f}"}} at m = 10, against {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s',0,'coverage'):.3f}"}}, {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s',3,'coverage'):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s',10,'coverage'):.3f}"}} with replay (this ablation used its own reference draws, so its LotCP-S values differ slightly from those of the main tables). The share of lots reaching 0.90 fell from {{f"{100*r.replay('lotcp_s',3,'meets'):.0f}"}} % to {{f"{100*r.replay('lotcp_s_noepisodic',3,'meets'):.0f}"}} % at m = 3. The interval score was not better with the replay ({{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s',10,'iscore'):.2f}"}} against {{f"{r.replay('lotcp_s_noepisodic',10,'iscore'):.2f}"}} at m = 10): the replay buys validity at small m, not efficiency.

*Episodic quantile.* Replacing the replayed quantile by the quantile of within-lot residuals centred on the true lot mean, which ignores the estimation error of the bias, gives coverage {{r.c('lotcp_noepisodic',3)}} at $m=3$ and {{r.c('lotcp_noepisodic',10)}} at $m=10$ for the location-only method, and it fails at $m=0$ ({{r.c('lotcp_noepisodic',0)}}). Because season-fold residuals are conservative for $m>0$, the omission is not punished in this data set. The replay is nevertheless what makes the scale-adaptive score calibratable at all, since the distribution of $|r-\hat b|/\hat s$ depends on $m$ through both estimates.

*Weighting.* Giving every scan rather than every lot the same weight changes little ({{r.c('lotcp_pooled',10)}} against {{r.c('lotcp',10)}} at $m=10$), because lot sizes are fairly homogeneous here.

In summary, H4 holds for the scale component and, at $m=0$, for the fold unit. It does not hold for shrinkage, which was therefore removed from the method, and holds only at very small $m$ for the episodic quantile of the location-only method.

## S5.2 Generalisation across predictors and protocols

Fig. 7 and supplementary table S21 repeat the comparison for LightGBM and the CNN, and for the leave-one-season-out protocol. The seed-to-seed standard deviation of lot-macro coverage is at most {{f"{r.t['table7b_seed_spread'].query('method in [\"lotcp_s\",\"lotcp\"]').coverage_sd.max():.3f}"}} for the LotCP variants (supplementary table S7b).


![Fig. 7. Lot-macro coverage of LotCP and LotCP-S for three point predictors and two protocols (nominal 0.90).](figures/fig7_generalisation.png){width=6.5in}

The failure of sample-fold split conformal prediction is larger for the more flexible predictors: coverage is {{f"{r.gen('gbm','forward','scp_sample',0):.3f}"}} for LightGBM and {{f"{r.gen('cnn','forward','scp_sample',0):.3f}"}} for the CNN, against {{r.c('scp_sample',0)}} for PLS, because flexible models absorb more of the lot effects of their training lots. For the same reason, bias correction with the source quantile, which over-covers with PLS, under-covers with LightGBM ({{f"{r.gen('gbm','forward','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$) and sits at {{f"{r.gen('cnn','forward','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} with the CNN. Its coverage depends on how strongly the predictor fits its training lots, which the user does not control. LotCP-S covers {{f"{r.gen('gbm','forward','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.gen('cnn','forward','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$ with these predictors, and {{f"{r.gen('pls','loso','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.gen('cnn','loso','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} under leave-one-season-out with PLS and the CNN. Across the five predictor–protocol combinations its coverage at $m=10$ stays within {{f"{min(r.gen(a,b,'lotcp_s',10) for a,b in [('pls','forward'),('gbm','forward'),('cnn','forward'),('pls','loso'),('cnn','loso')]):.3f}"}}–{{f"{max(r.gen(a,b,'lotcp_s',10) for a,b in [('pls','forward'),('gbm','forward'),('cnn','forward'),('pls','loso'),('cnn','loso')]):.3f}"}}. With the CNN, whose point error is lowest, LotCP-S at $m=10$ gives a mean width of {{f"{r.gen('cnn','forward','lotcp_s',10,'width'):.2f}"}} % DMC, that is, about ±{{f"{r.gen('cnn','forward','lotcp_s',10,'width')/2:.1f}"}} % DMC.

## S5.3 External validation on soil vis–NIR spectra

The experiments so far use one fruit and one instrument family. We therefore repeated the central comparisons on two soil subsets of the Open Soil Spectral Library (Section 3), a different material, spectral range, instrument and reference chemistry, in a field where adjusting a library model with a few local samples is established practice. Table 12 and Fig. 10 give the results.

{{T:soil}}

![Fig. 10. Lot-macro coverage on the soil data as a function of the number of local reference samples (PLS, nominal 0.90, dotted line). Dashed line: split conformal prediction with sample folds and no reference samples.](figures/fig10_soil.png){width=6.5in}

*The coverage loss is larger than for mango.* A model trained on the 2009 LUCAS campaign and applied to the 2015 campaign has a prediction error on the target of {{f"{r.soilp('lucas_oc','rmse_target'):.2f}"}} log-units for organic carbon, against {{f"{r.soilp('lucas_oc','rmse_cv_sample'):.2f}"}} in cross-validation, with a mean offset of {{f"{r.soilp('lucas_oc','bias_target'):.2f}"}}; for pH the error is {{f"{r.soilp('lucas_ph','rmse_target'):.2f}"}} against {{f"{r.soilp('lucas_ph','rmse_cv_sample'):.2f}"}}, with an offset of {{f"{r.soilp('lucas_ph','bias_target'):.2f}"}} pH units. We do not know the origin of this between-campaign offset; whatever its cause, it is the kind of batch effect a user of a spectral library can meet. Split conformal intervals calibrated within the 2009 campaign then cover {{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','scp_sample',0):.2f}"}} (organic carbon) and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','scp_sample',0):.2f}"}} (pH) of the 2015 values in {{int(r.soil('lucas_oc','scp_sample',0,'n_lots'))}} grid cells, at a nominal 0.90. Holding residuals out by lot does not help ({{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','scp_lot',0):.2f}"}} and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','scp_lot',0):.2f}"}}), because the shift is between campaigns and the source contains only one. This is the situation in which no label-free repair exists. For held-out KSSL projects, which are exchangeable with the calibration projects by construction, sample-fold split conformal prediction covers {{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','scp_sample',0):.3f}"}} and lot-fold calibration {{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','scp_lot',0):.3f}"}}: with exchangeable lots and no campaign effect, the loss is small and holding out lots removes it, as hierarchical conformal theory predicts.

*A few local reference samples repair most of it.* With five reference samples per cell, LotCP-S covers {{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','lotcp_s',5):.3f}"}} for organic carbon and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s',5):.3f}"}} for pH; with twenty, {{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','lotcp_s',20):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s',20):.3f}"}}. Coverage stays up to three points below nominal, because the residual scale of the 2015 campaign also differs from that of the 2009 cells that calibrate the half-width; the method does not fully absorb a shift in scale between campaigns. For KSSL projects it covers {{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','lotcp_s',5):.3f}"}}–{{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}.

*Comparison with the alternatives.* On LUCAS organic carbon LotCP-S has a lower interval score than bias correction with the source quantile and slope/bias correction at $m=5$, 10 and 20, and than centred target-only calibration at $m=10$ and 20 (Holm-adjusted p ≤ {{f"{max(float(r.soiltest('lucas_oc',o,m).p_holm) for o in ('biascorr_naive','slopebias_naive') for m in (5,10,20)) + 0.0005:.3f}"}} against the first two; p {{r.soilpv('lucas_oc','target_only_centred',10)}} and {{r.soilpv('lucas_oc','target_only_centred',20)}} against the third; supplementary table S13c). On LUCAS pH it is better than bias correction at $m=5$ and 10 (p {{r.soilpv('lucas_ph','biascorr_naive',5)}} and {{r.soilpv('lucas_ph','biascorr_naive',10)}}) and not different at $m=20$ (p {{r.soilpv('lucas_ph','biascorr_naive',20)}}); slope/bias correction has a numerically lower score from $m=10$, a difference that is not significant. On KSSL it does not differ from bias correction at $m=5$ and 10 (p {{r.soilpv('kssl_oc','biascorr_naive',5)}} and {{r.soilpv('kssl_oc','biascorr_naive',10)}}) and is better at $m=20$ (p {{r.soilpv('kssl_oc','biascorr_naive',20)}}); slope/bias correction has the numerically best score there at $m=20$. Bias correction with the source quantile under-covers on all three soil tasks ({{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}}, {{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$), where it over-covered with PLS on mango: its coverage is again not under control. Centred target-only calibration is valid and wider, as before. Since soil samples have a single spectrum, these comparisons involve no replicate-scan information; the advantage of LotCP-S on LUCAS comes from calibrating the half-width for the estimation error and from the reference-sample scale.

*Shrinkage fails under a campaign shift.* The pre-specified shrinkage variant covers only {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s_shrink',5):.2f}"}} for pH and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','lotcp_s_shrink',5):.2f}"}} for organic carbon at $m=5$, against {{f"{r.soil('lucas_ph','lotcp_s',5):.2f}"}} and {{f"{r.soil('lucas_oc','lotcp_s',5):.2f}"}} with the plain mean (supplementary table S13). The between-lot variance estimated inside the 2009 campaign is small, so the shrinkage weight is low and the estimate is pulled towards the 2009 mean, which the 2015 campaign has left. On KSSL, where lots are exchangeable, shrinkage gives a small gain at $m=5$ (interval score {{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','lotcp_s_shrink',5,'iscore'):.2f}"}} against {{f"{r.soil('kssl_oc','lotcp_s',5,'iscore'):.2f}"}}). Shrinkage thus helps a little when its assumption holds and fails badly when it does not, and the user cannot know in advance which case applies. This is why the reported method does not use it.

Taken together, the soil data reproduce the three main findings on independent material: intervals calibrated within the source population lose coverage on a new population, drastically so across survey campaigns; a handful of local reference samples restores most of the coverage; and LotCP-S is not significantly worse than any competing method at $m\le10$ and is significantly better where the shift is large. They also show a limit: under a between-campaign change of residual scale, coverage remains a few points short of nominal.

## S5.4 Robustness, computational cost and failure analysis

The detailed analyses are in Supplementary Notes S1–S3; the main results are these. *Robustness* (Note S1, Fig. S2, table S22). Adding Gaussian error with a standard deviation of 0.5 % DMC to the reference values left LotCP-S coverage at {{f"{r.robcov('label_noise_sd',0.5,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$ and widened the intervals by {{r.pct(r.robcov('label_noise_sd',0.5,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} %. Removing provider-flagged outliers changed coverage by {{f"{abs(r.robcov('outliers_removed',1,'lotcp_s',10)-r.m('lotcp_s',10)):.3f}"}}. With only 20 past lots the method was conservative ({{f"{r.robcov('n_cal_lots',20,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}, intervals {{r.pct(r.robcov('n_cal_lots',20,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % wider), and about 40 past lots were needed before the conservatism became small. Episode count and pseudo-count were immaterial. When the deployment unit was a single instrument unit within a lot, coverage was {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}. When only the two preceding seasons were available it was {{f"{r.robcov('protocol','recent2','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}, and for a held-out region or cultivar {{f"{r.robcov('protocol','region','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.robcov('protocol','cultivar','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}. Results at nominal levels 0.80 and 0.95 follow the same pattern (table S23). *Cost* (Note S2, table S24). The episodic calibration takes about {{f"{r.cost['lotcp_s_m10']['calibration_seconds']:.1f}"}} s for {{r.cost['lotcp_s_m10']['n_cal_lots']}} past lots, and deployment computes one bias and one scale per lot. The binding cost is the number of destructive reference tests, which Section 4.7 and Supplementary Note S7 address. *Failures* (Note S3, Fig. S3). At $m=10$, {{r.lotfail('scp_sample')}} of the {{r.nlot_err()}} target lots had coverage below 0.80 under sample-fold split conformal prediction, {{r.lotfail('lotcp')}} under LotCP and {{r.lotfail('lotcp_s')}} under LotCP-S. After bias correction the remaining failures were lots whose residual spread exceeded what past lots suggested (Spearman correlation of lot coverage with the ratio of lot to prior residual standard deviation, {{r.rho('lotcp_s','ratio')}}). Coverage was lower for fruit at the ripening stage ({{r.sub('physio_stage','ripen','lotcp_s')}} against {{r.sub('physio_stage','green','lotcp_s')}}), for the cultivar Keitt ({{r.sub('cultivar','keitt','lotcp_s')}}) and at the extremes of the DMC range.

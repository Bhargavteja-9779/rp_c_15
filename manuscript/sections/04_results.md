# 4. Results

## 4.1 Point accuracy and the structure of the error

On the data providers' published split (three seasons for training, n = {{r.repro_n_train()}}; the fourth season as test set, n = {{int(r.repro('pls_published','val_ext','n'))}}), the published PLS recipe reached an RMSEP of {{f"{r.repro('pls_published','val_ext'):.3f}"}} % DMC, against 1.01 % reported by Anderson et al. [@anderson2020]. Our SNV PLS reached {{f"{r.repro('pls_ours','val_ext'):.3f}"}} %, and the CNN without augmentation {{f"{r.repro('cnn_ours','val_ext','rmsep','min'):.2f}"}}–{{f"{r.repro('cnn_ours','val_ext','rmsep','max'):.2f}"}} % against 0.77 % for a tuned and augmented CNN [@walsh2024]. Both PLS recipes are therefore reported below.

Under forward chaining, the error on the next season exceeded the sample-fold cross-validation estimate in every target season (Table 2). The standard deviation of the lot mean residual within a target season was {{f"{r.t['table2_point_models'].query('model==\"pls\"').sd_lot_bias.min():.2f}"}}–{{f"{r.t['table2_point_models'].query('model==\"pls\"').sd_lot_bias.max():.2f}"}} % DMC, of the same order as the within-lot error. Lots scatter around season-specific offsets (Fig. 2). The season-held-out variance components put the between-lot standard deviation at {{r.tau_sigma()['tau']}} % DMC and the within-lot, between-sample standard deviation at {{r.tau_sigma()['sigma']}} % DMC.

## 4.2 Error bars without reference fruit

Split conformal prediction with sample-grouped folds covered {{r.c('scp_sample',0)}} of reference values on later-season lots at a nominal 0.90 ({{f"{r.ss('scp_sample',0,'coverage_ex2017'):.3f}"}} without 2017) and reached 0.90 in none of the five seasons (Table 3). The Gaussian band, the quantile forest and conformalised quantile regression with sample folds behaved alike ({{r.c('gauss',0)}}, {{r.c('qrf',0)}}, {{r.c('cqr_sample',0)}}). The deep ensemble and Monte-Carlo dropout gave the lowest coverage ({{r.c('ensemble',0)}}, {{r.c('mc_dropout',0)}}), because their predictive variance describes the training seasons. With the published PLS recipe, sample-fold split conformal prediction covered {{f"{r.ss('scp_sample',0,'coverage_pooled','plspub'):.3f}"}}.

Holding out whole seasons is the honest label-free option: pooled coverage became {{r.c('scp_season',0)}}, at {{r.pct(r.m('scp_season',0,'width'), r.m('scp_sample',0,'width'))}} % more width. The pooled figure, however, is carried by 2017. Coverage by season ranged over {{r.season_range('scp_season',0)}}, the season mean was {{f"{r.ss('scp_season',0,'coverage_season_avg'):.3f}"}}, and without 2017 it was {{f"{r.ss('scp_season',0,'coverage_ex2017'):.3f}"}}. In 2017 only two source seasons were available, the season-held-out models were weak and the intervals were too wide. With four to six past seasons, the next one is not exchangeable with them to within a few coverage points. Weighting calibration points by the likelihood ratio of the unlabelled target spectra reached {{r.c('wcp',0)}} coverage but returned unbounded intervals for {{f"{100*r.m('wcp',0,'frac_infinite'):.0f}"}} % of scans; the shift that matters is a bias in the residual, which reweighting spectra does not repair.

## 4.3 Error bars with reference fruit

With five to ten reference fruit per lot, LotCP-S raised coverage over sample-fold split conformal prediction by {{f"{r.st('lotcp_s','scp_sample@m0','coverage',5):.2f}"}}–{{f"{r.st('lotcp_s','scp_sample@m0','coverage',10):.2f}"}} (season mean) in all five seasons, with a season-cluster confidence interval excluding zero, for both PLS recipes (Table 4, Fig. 3). Its pooled coverage at $m=10$ was {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}, its season mean {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'coverage_season_avg'):.3f}"}}, and without 2017 {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'coverage_ex2017'):.3f}"}}. By season, coverage was {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'cov_2017'):.3f}"}}, {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'cov_2018'):.3f}"}}, {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'cov_2019'):.3f}"}}, {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'cov_2020'):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'cov_2021'):.3f}"}} for 2017–2021. LotCP-S therefore removed most of the shortfall but stayed slightly below nominal in the seasons with an adequate source set, and its gain over season-fold split conformal prediction without reference fruit was not distinguishable at season level (Table 4). Excluding 2018, the season used to design the lot scale, gives {{f"{r.ss('lotcp_s',10,'coverage_ex2018'):.3f}"}}.

*What the replay buys.* The matched baseline (b) uses the same bias and lot scale as LotCP-S and differs only in how the multiplier is calibrated. It covered {{f"{r.ss('biascorr_full',5):.3f}"}} at $m=5$ and {{f"{r.ss('biascorr_full',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$ ({{f"{r.ss('biascorr_full',10,'coverage_ex2017'):.3f}"}} without 2017), {{f"{100*r.st('lotcp_s','biascorr_full','coverage',3):.1f}"}}, {{f"{100*r.st('lotcp_s','biascorr_full','coverage',5):.1f}"}} and {{f"{100*r.st('lotcp_s','biascorr_full','coverage',10):.1f}"}} points less than LotCP-S at $m=3$, 5 and 10, with confidence intervals excluding zero for both PLS recipes. Its interval score was equal or slightly lower (by {{f"{-r.st('lotcp_s','biascorr_full','iscore',10):.2f}"}} at $m=10$; season-cluster interval for the advantage of LotCP-S {{r.stci('lotcp_s','biascorr_full','iscore',10,digits=2)}}). Replaying the $m$-shot procedure on past lots thus prices the error of estimating the bias and scale from a few fruit. It protects coverage when $m$ is small. It does not make intervals narrower. The lot scale is what narrows them. Against the replicate-scan scale alone (b′), LotCP-S had a lower interval score (intervals excluding zero for $m\ge5$) and at least equal coverage.

*Current practice.* Bias correction with the sample-fold half-width over-covered ({{f"{r.ss('biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$) because a remnant of the lot effect in sample-fold residuals offsets the ignored estimation error of the bias. Its half-width cannot shrink with $m$, and LotCP-S had a lower interval score at season level from $m=10$ with SNV PLS ({{r.stci('lotcp_s','biascorr_naive','iscore',10,digits=2)}}) and from $m=3$ with the published recipe, at the price of lower coverage. In the pre-specified confirmation on independent soil data, the interval-score advantage over this baseline was not confirmed (Section 4.5). Target-only calibration on the reference fruit alone needs $m\ge9$, was conservative and {{r.pct(r.m('target_only_centred',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % wider than LotCP-S at $m=10$, and matched its interval score by $m=20$ ({{r.i('target_only_centred',20)}} against {{r.i('lotcp_s',20)}}). The other predictors, protocols, nominal levels, reference-value noise and the component ablation are in Supplementary Note S5. Without repeated scans per fruit, LotCP-S and bias correction did not differ significantly in interval score (supplementary table S6b).

## 4.4 Lot-level assurance

Near-nominal average coverage still leaves many lots below the nominal level: with LotCP-S at $m=10$ only {{r.meet('lotcp_s',10)}} % of lot-draws reached 0.90 (Fig. 4). LotCP-A, asked for 0.90 coverage in nine lots of ten, brought this share to {{r.meet('lotcp_a',10)}} % pooled at $m=10$, but {{f"{100*r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'meets_ex2017'):.1f}"}} % without 2017. By season it was {{f"{100*r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'meets_2017'):.0f}"}}, {{f"{100*r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'meets_2018'):.0f}"}}, {{f"{100*r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'meets_2019'):.0f}"}}, {{f"{100*r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'meets_2020'):.0f}"}} and {{f"{100*r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'meets_2021'):.0f}"}} % for 2017–2021: the target was met in {{int(r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'seasons_meeting_assurance'))}} of five seasons with SNV PLS and in {{int(r.ss('lotcp_a',10,'seasons_meeting_assurance','plspub'))}} with the published recipe. The lot-level guarantee rests on exchangeable lots, and season shift breaks it in the same way as it breaks the marginal one. LotCP-A did raise the share relative to LotCP-S in all five seasons (Table 4), at {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_a',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % more width at $m=10$.

The extra width buys no efficiency. Measured as the share of lots whose draw-averaged coverage reaches 0.90, LotCP-A at $m=10$ ({{f"{100*r.af('lotcp_a',10):.1f}"}} %, width {{f"{r.af('lotcp_a',10,'width'):.2f}"}}) is matched by LotCP-S run at $\alpha=0.05$ ({{f"{100*r.af('lotcp_s_a0.05',10):.1f}"}} %, width {{f"{r.af('lotcp_s_a0.05',10,'width'):.2f}"}}). LotCP-A is a way of choosing that level from past lots without target labels. It is not a cheaper route to assurance.

## 4.6 Harvest decisions (exploratory)

At the operating point fixed in advance, deciding on point predictions cost {{f"{r.dc('point',0):.1f}"}} units per 100 fruit under the assumed costs (SNV PLS; Table 5). Any calibrated lower bound cost about {{f"{r.dd('point',0):.0f}"}} units less: the split-conformal bound without reference fruit {{f"{r.dc('scp_lower',0):.2f}"}} [{{f"{r.dc('scp_lower',0,'cost100_lo'):.2f}"}}, {{f"{r.dc('scp_lower',0,'cost100_hi'):.2f}"}}], The difference to point decisions had a confidence interval excluding zero and favoured the bound in all five seasons, for both PLS recipes. Reference fruit did not lower the cost further. The LotCP-S bound with $m=3$, 5 and 10 differed from the bound without reference fruit by {{f"{r.dd('lotcp_s_lower',3):+.2f}"}}, {{f"{r.dd('lotcp_s_lower',5):+.2f}"}} and {{f"{r.dd('lotcp_s_lower',10):+.2f}"}} units, all with intervals including zero, and bias correction with a source margin behaved alike. The bound accepted far fewer immature fruit than the point prediction ({{f"{r.dc('lotcp_s_lower',5,'immature_accepted'):.1f}"}} against {{f"{r.dc('point',0,'immature_accepted'):.1f}"}} per 100 fruit at $m=5$) and withheld more mature fruit ({{f"{r.dc('lotcp_s_lower',5,'mature_withheld'):.0f}"}} against {{f"{r.dc('point',0,'mature_withheld'):.0f}"}}).

At lot level the point rule harvested {{f"{r.dc('point',0,'premature_lots'):.1f}"}} % of not-ready lots [{{f"{r.dc('point',0,'premature_lo'):.1f}"}}, {{f"{r.dc('point',0,'premature_hi'):.1f}"}}] and delayed {{f"{r.dc('point',0,'delayed_lots'):.0f}"}} % of ready lots. The bound without reference fruit harvested {{f"{r.dc('scp_lower',0,'premature_lots'):.1f}"}} % of not-ready lots and delayed {{f"{r.dc('scp_lower',0,'delayed_lots'):.0f}"}} % of ready lots; with five reference fruit, LotCP-S harvested {{f"{r.dc('lotcp_s_lower',5,'premature_lots'):.1f}"}} % and delayed {{f"{r.dc('lotcp_s_lower',5,'delayed_lots'):.0f}"}} %. The confidence intervals overlap: {{int(r.dc('scp_lower',0,'not_ready_lots'))}} not-ready and {{int(r.dc('scp_lower',0,'ready_lots'))}} ready lots in five seasons cannot resolve differences of this size. Choosing $\alpha$ by leave-one-season-out cost minimisation was not better than the cost-derived level $\alpha^\ast$ (LotCP-S, $m=10$: {{f"{r.al('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.1,'cost_loso'):.2f}"}} against {{f"{r.al('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.1,'cost_alpha_star'):.2f}"}} units at $\alpha^\ast=0.10$). Full trade-off curves are in Fig. 5 and Supplementary Note S7.

## 4.7 Planning the number of reference fruit

The width planned from past lots matched the realised width with a median absolute relative difference of {{f"{100*r.plan.rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} % over all predictors, seasons and values of $m$ (Fig. 6). The error depended on the number of source seasons: LotCP-S widths were over-forecast by {{f"{-100*r.pbs(2):.0f}"}} % with two source seasons (2017) and within {{f"{100*max(abs(r.pbs(k)) for k in (3,4,5,6)):.0f}"}} % with three to six. Most of the reduction in width came from the first five reference fruit; beyond ten the curve was flat.

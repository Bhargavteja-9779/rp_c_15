Fig. 1. Risk-aware harvest workflow. Before the season, past lots are used to calibrate the few-shot correction and to plan the number of reference fruit. In the orchard, fruit of a new lot are scanned, m of them are tested destructively, and each fruit receives a calibrated lower bound that is compared with the dry-matter specification.

Fig. 2. The data set. (a) Reference dry matter content by season. (b) Number of harvest lots by season. (c) Mean absorbance spectra of three seasons in the analysis window.

Fig. 3. Why calibration on held-out samples fails (PLS, forward chaining). (a) Residuals of sample-fold cross-validation in the source seasons and residuals on the following season, pooled over the five target seasons. (b) Mean residual of each target lot; the bar marks the season mean.

Fig. 4. Lot-macro coverage, mean width and interval score as a function of the number of reference samples per lot (PLS, forward chaining, 137 target lots, nominal 0.90). Dashed lines: split conformal prediction without reference samples.

Fig. 5. Coverage within each of the 137 target lots (PLS, nominal 0.90, dotted line). Marginal coverage near the nominal level can coexist with many badly covered lots.

Fig. 6. Lot-level assurance (PLS, forward chaining). (a) Share of lot-draws in which at least 90 % of the fruit is covered; dotted lines mark the targets of LotCP-A for δ = 0.10 and δ = 0.20. (b) Mean interval width.

Fig. 7. Trade-off between harvesting immature fruit and holding back mature fruit (PLS, specification 15 % DMC, 137 later-season lots). Each curve traces one rule as its confidence level α varies from 0.02 to 0.5; crosses are rules without a confidence level. (a) Fruit-level decision. (b) Lot-level decision: a lot is harvested when at least 80 % of its scanned fruit are accepted.

Fig. 8. Expected cost under assumed cost ratios (PLS, specification 15 % DMC). (a) Cost per 100 fruit, in units of the value of one marketable fruit, for five decision rules as the assumed cost of withholding a mature fruit varies; the LotCP-S bound uses five reference fruit and the cost-derived level α*. (b) Cost of the LotCP-S rule as a function of the number of reference fruit for three lot sizes at c_D = 1/9; circles mark the minimum.

Fig. 9. Reference-budget planning. (a) Interval width planned from past lots against the mean width realised on the lots of the following season; each point is one target season, predictor, number of reference samples and variant. (b) Planned and realised budget curves for PLS, averaged over target seasons.

Fig. 10. Ablation: lot-macro coverage with bootstrap 95 % CI for m = 5 and m = 20; the interval score (IS) is printed next to each variant.

Fig. 11. Lot-macro coverage of LotCP and LotCP-S for three point predictors and two protocols (nominal 0.90).

Fig. 12. Lot-macro coverage on the soil data as a function of the number of local reference samples (PLS, nominal 0.90, dotted line). Dashed line: split conformal prediction with sample folds and no reference samples.

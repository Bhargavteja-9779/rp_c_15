## 6.2 Intervals without reference samples (RQ1)

Table 3 lists the intervals that need no reference sample. Split conformal prediction with sample-grouped folds covers {{r.c('scp_sample',0)}} of the reference values on later-season lots (95 % CI {{r.ci('scp_sample',0)}}) at a nominal 0.90, and {{r.u('scp_sample',0)}} % of the lots have coverage below 0.80. The Gaussian band, the quantile regression forest and conformalised quantile regression with sample folds behave similarly (coverage {{r.c('gauss',0)}}, {{r.c('qrf',0)}} and {{r.c('cqr_sample',0)}}). The deep ensemble and Monte-Carlo dropout give the narrowest intervals and the lowest coverage ({{r.c('ensemble',0)}} and {{r.c('mc_dropout',0)}}): their predictive variance reflects the training seasons, not the lot effect of a new one. Their interval score is nevertheless the best in the table, because the CNN underlying them has a lower point error than PLS (Table 2). The shortfall is not produced by the lot-balanced weighting: with every scan weighted equally, sample-fold split conformal prediction covers {{r.unweighted('scp_sample')}}. H1 is confirmed.

{{T:main0}}

The hold-out unit orders coverage as hypothesised (H2): sample folds {{r.c('scp_sample',0)}}, lot folds {{r.c('scp_lot',0)}}, season folds {{r.c('scp_season',0)}}, and the ordering holds in each of the five target seasons (Table 5). Holding out lots repairs only a small part of the gap, which shows that the lot effect within the training seasons is smaller than the lot effect of a new season. Season-held-out residuals bring the pooled coverage to the nominal level, for split conformal prediction and for conformalised quantile regression alike ({{r.c('cqr_season',0)}}). Two qualifications are needed. First, the price is width: intervals are {{r.pct(r.m('scp_season',0,'width'), r.m('scp_sample',0,'width'))}} % wider than with sample folds, and the interval score is not better ({{r.i('scp_season',0)}} against {{r.i('scp_sample',0)}}). Second, the pooled figure hides season-to-season variation: coverage by season ranges over {{r.season_range('scp_season',0)}} (Table 5), with over-coverage in 2017, when only two source seasons were available and the season-held-out models were weak, and coverage below nominal in each of the four later seasons. With a handful of past seasons, the next season is simply not exchangeable with them to within a few coverage points. LotCP and LotCP-S at $m=0$ behave like season-fold split conformal prediction, as expected.

Weighted conformal prediction with likelihood-ratio weights from the unlabelled target spectra reaches {{r.c('wcp',0)}} coverage, but with intervals {{r.pct(r.m('wcp',0,'width'), r.m('scp_sample',0,'width'))}} % wider than split conformal prediction and unbounded intervals for {{f"{100*r.m('wcp',0,'frac_infinite'):.0f}"}} % of scans: when a lot is spectrally distinct, the estimated weights concentrate on few calibration points. It has the worst interval score of all methods. Covariate-shift weighting is not the right tool here, because the shift that matters is a lot bias in the residual, not a change in the distribution of spectra alone.

## 6.3 Intervals with reference samples (RQ2)

Table 4 and Fig. 4 show what $m$ reference samples per lot buy. Three samples are enough to change the picture: every method that corrects the lot bias then covers at least 0.90 with intervals of about the same width as the invalid sample-fold split conformal intervals. The corrected point prediction improves accordingly: the lot-level RMSE of PLS falls from {{f"{r.point(0,'none'):.2f}"}} % DMC without correction to {{f"{r.point(3,'shrinkage'):.2f}"}} with three and {{f"{r.point(10,'shrinkage'):.2f}"}} with ten reference samples.

{{T:mainm}}

![Fig. 4. Lot-macro coverage, mean width and interval score as a function of the number of reference samples per lot (PLS, forward chaining, 137 target lots, nominal 0.90). Dashed lines: split conformal prediction without reference samples.](figures/fig4_main.png){width=6.5in}

*Methods that do not correct the bias.* Pooling the reference residuals into the source calibration set has no visible effect (coverage {{r.c('pooled',10)}} at $m=10$): a few target residuals among tens of thousands of source residuals do not move the quantile. Adaptive conformal inference over the reference samples raises coverage to {{r.c('aci',10)}} at $m=10$ but does so by widening the interval, and returns unbounded intervals in {{f"{100*r.m('aci',10,'frac_infinite'):.0f}"}} % of lot-draws.

*Target-only calibration.* Split conformal prediction on the reference samples alone is valid within each lot, which shows in its tight distribution of lot-wise coverage (Fig. 5). It cannot return a bounded 90 % interval for $m<9$, and at $m=10$ it is conservative (coverage {{r.c('target_only_centred',10)}}) and wide: the centred version is {{r.pct(r.m('target_only_centred',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % wider than LotCP-S. By $m=20$ the centred version reaches the interval score of LotCP-S ({{r.i('target_only_centred',20)}} against {{r.i('lotcp_s',20)}}) and at $m=30$ it is marginally better ({{r.i('target_only_centred',30)}} against {{r.i('lotcp_s',30)}}).

*Bias correction with the source quantile.* Subtracting the mean reference residual and keeping the sample-fold half-width is a strong baseline with PLS. Its coverage is above nominal ({{r.c('biascorr_naive',5)}}–{{r.c('biascorr_naive',20)}}) because two errors partly cancel: the sample-fold residuals contain a remnant of the lot effect, which widens the interval, and the uncertainty of the estimated bias is ignored, which should narrow it. The half-width does not depend on $m$, so the method cannot turn additional reference samples into narrower intervals, and Section 8 shows that the cancellation does not carry over to other predictors.

*Slope-and-bias correction and spiking.* Two further maintenance practices were tested. Regressing the reference values of the $m$ samples on their predictions and applying the fitted slope and intercept is worse than the mean correction at $m=5$ (interval score {{r.i('slopebias_naive',5)}} against {{r.i('biascorr_naive',5)}}), because five points do not determine a slope, and is comparable from $m=10$ ({{r.i('slopebias_naive',10)}} against {{r.i('biascorr_naive',10)}}). Refitting the PLS model with ten reference samples added to the training set ("spiking") barely changes the predictions when the samples are added once (lot-level RMSE {{f"{r.spk('spiking_x1'):.2f}"}} % DMC against {{f"{r.spk('none'):.2f}"}} without any correction) and remains clearly worse than the mean bias correction when they are replicated twenty times ({{f"{r.spk('spiking_x20'):.2f}"}} against {{f"{r.spk('bias_correction'):.2f}"}}; supplementary table S11).

*LotCP and LotCP-S.* LotCP covers {{f"{min(r.m('lotcp',m) for m in (3,5,10,20,30)):.3f}"}}–{{f"{max(r.m('lotcp',m) for m in (3,5,10,20,30)):.3f}"}}, somewhat above nominal, with a half-width that shrinks as $m$ grows. LotCP-S covers {{f"{min(r.m('lotcp_s',m) for m in (3,5,10,20,30)):.3f}"}}–{{f"{max(r.m('lotcp_s',m) for m in (3,5,10,20,30)):.3f}"}} for $m\ge3$ and has the lowest interval score of all methods for $m$ from 3 to 20 (Table 4). At $m=10$ its intervals are {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('biascorr_naive',10,'width'))}} % narrower than those of bias correction with the source quantile and {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp',10,'width'))}} % narrower than those of LotCP. By season, its coverage at $m=10$ ranges over {{r.season_range('lotcp_s',10)}} (Table 5). Leaving out one target season at a time moves the pooled figure between {{f"{r.jk('lotcp_s',10,'jackknife_min'):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.jk('lotcp_s',10,'jackknife_max'):.3f}"}}, and the unweighted mean over seasons is {{f"{r.jk('lotcp_s',10,'season_mean_unweighted'):.3f}"}} (supplementary table S4b); for sample-fold split conformal prediction the corresponding figures are {{f"{r.jk('scp_sample',0,'jackknife_min'):.3f}"}}–{{f"{r.jk('scp_sample',0,'jackknife_max'):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.jk('scp_sample',0,'season_mean_unweighted'):.3f}"}}. The 2018 value is below nominal even though 2018 served as development data for the scale estimator; we return to this in Section 11.

![Fig. 5. Coverage within each of the 137 target lots (PLS, nominal 0.90, dotted line). Marginal coverage near the nominal level can coexist with many badly covered lots.](figures/fig5_lot_coverage.png){width=6.5in}

*Paired tests.* Table 7 compares the per-lot interval score of LotCP-S with its competitors. Against bias correction with the source quantile the difference is not significant at $m=3$ (Holm-adjusted p {{r.p('biascorr_naive',3)}}) and is significant from $m=5$ on (p {{r.p('biascorr_naive',5)}} at $m=5$, p {{r.p('biascorr_naive',10)}} at $m=10$), with the mean difference favouring LotCP-S in all five seasons from $m=5$. Against the centred target-only method LotCP-S is better at $m=10$ (p {{r.p('target_only_centred',10)}}), marginally better at $m=20$ (p {{r.p('target_only_centred',20)}}, mean difference {{f"{r.test('target_only_centred',20).mean_diff:.2f}"}}) and not different at $m=30$ (p {{r.p('target_only_centred',30)}}). # 7. Ablation study (RQ3)

Table 6 and Fig. 6 remove or replace one component at a time.

{{T:ablation}}

![Fig. 6. Ablation: lot-macro coverage with bootstrap 95 % CI for m = 5 and m = 20; the interval score (IS) is printed next to each variant.](figures/fig6_ablation.png){width=6.3in}

*Scale.* The lot-specific scale is the component that matters most once $m>0$. Removing it (LotCP) raises the interval score at $m=10$ from {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}} to {{r.i('lotcp',10)}}. The two sources of scale information both contribute: with the reference-sample variance alone the score is {{r.i('lotcp_s_support',10)}}, with the replicate-scan variance alone {{r.i('lotcp_s_rep',10)}}. The replicate-scan term is the only one available at $m\le1$ and costs no reference measurement. It is also what separates LotCP-S from the bias-correction shortcut when reference samples are few. Without the replicate-scan term, the per-lot interval score of the reference-scale-only variant does not differ significantly from that of bias correction with the source quantile at $m=3$, 5 or 10 (Holm-adjusted p {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',3)}}, {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',5)}} and {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',10)}}) and is better only from $m=20$ (p {{r.pb('biascorr_naive',20)}}; supplementary table S6b). A user who takes a single scan per fruit should therefore not expect an efficiency gain over that shortcut with fewer than about twenty reference samples; what remains is the stability of coverage across predictors shown in Section 8.

*Fold unit.* At $m=0$, residuals held out by lot or by sample give coverage {{r.c('lotcp_lotfold',0)}} and {{r.c('lotcp_samplefold',0)}}; season-held-out residuals are needed to reach the nominal level. With $m>0$ the picture changes. After the lot bias has been corrected, what is left to calibrate is the within-lot residual, and lot-fold residuals describe it well: LotCP on lot-fold residuals covers {{r.c('lotcp_lotfold',10)}} at $m=10$, slightly below nominal, with narrower intervals than LotCP on season-fold residuals, which over-covers ({{r.c('lotcp',10)}}). LotCP-S on lot-fold residuals covers {{r.c('lotcp_s_lotfold',10)}} with an interval score of {{r.i('lotcp_s_lotfold',10)}}, against {{r.c('lotcp_s',10)}} and {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}} for season-fold residuals. The lot-fold variant of LotCP-S is therefore the more efficient one, and it is not less safe by season: its lowest per-season coverage at $m=10$ is {{r.season_min('lotcp_s_lotfold',10)}}, against {{r.season_min('lotcp_s',10)}} for season-fold residuals. Season-held-out residuals are thus needed without reference samples and are not needed with them. We keep the season-fold variant as the reported method because it was fixed before this comparison was made, and note that the choice costs about {{r.pct(r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s_lotfold',10,'width'))}} % in width at $m=10$.

*Shrinkage.* Shrinking the bias estimate has a small effect at $m=3$ (interval score {{r.i('lotcp',3)}} against {{r.i('lotcp_noshrink',3)}} for the plain mean) and none from $m=10$. The reason is visible in the variance components: the lot bias is large relative to the sample noise, so the shrinkage weight is already {{r.tau_sigma()['w3']}} at $m=3$. The part of H4 that concerns shrinkage is not supported; in this data set the plain mean of Lee et al.'s remark is sufficient.

*Episodic quantile.* Replacing the replayed quantile by the quantile of within-lot residuals centred on the true lot mean, which ignores the estimation error of the bias, gives coverage {{r.c('lotcp_noepisodic',3)}} at $m=3$ and {{r.c('lotcp_noepisodic',10)}} at $m=10$ for the location-only method, and it fails at $m=0$ ({{r.c('lotcp_noepisodic',0)}}). Because season-fold residuals are conservative for $m>0$, the omission is not punished in this data set. The replay is nevertheless what makes the scale-adaptive score calibratable at all, since the distribution of $|r-\hat b|/\hat s$ depends on $m$ through both estimates.

*Weighting.* Giving every scan rather than every lot the same weight changes little ({{r.c('lotcp_pooled',10)}} against {{r.c('lotcp',10)}} at $m=10$), because lot sizes are fairly homogeneous here.

In summary, H4 holds for the scale component and, at $m=0$, for the fold unit. It does not hold for shrinkage, and holds only at very small $m$ for the episodic quantile of the location-only method.

# 9. Robustness and sensitivity (RQ4)

{{T:robust}}

![Fig. 8. Robustness of LotCP-S coverage (PLS, nominal 0.90): (a) Gaussian error added to the reference values of the m reference samples; (b) number of past lots available for calibration; (c) other source–target shifts.](figures/fig8_robustness.png){width=6.5in}

*Error in the reference values.* Oven-drying has its own error, and the $m$ reference values carry it. Adding Gaussian error with a standard deviation of 0.25 or 0.5 % DMC to the reference samples at deployment (not to the calibration lots) leaves coverage at or slightly above nominal ({{f"{r.robcov('label_noise_sd',0.5,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$ for 0.5 % DMC) and widens the intervals by {{r.pct(r.robcov('label_noise_sd',0.5,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % (Table 9). The noisier references inflate the estimated lot scale, which errs on the conservative side.

*Reuse of the calibration lots.* The variance components and the pseudo-count are estimated from the same past lots that provide the conformal quantile. Estimating them on a random half of the lots and computing the quantile on the other half, with the pseudo-count fixed at 10, gives coverage {{f"{r.robf('split_constants','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and an interval score of {{f"{r.robf('split_constants','lotcp_s',10,'iscore'):.2f}"}} at $m=10$, against {{r.c('lotcp_s',10)}} and {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}}; the reuse does not make the intervals optimistic.

*Single-instrument units.* In the data set a lot was usually scanned with several instrument units, whereas a grower would use one. Redefining the deployment unit as one instrument unit within a lot (reference and query scans from that unit only; {{int(r.rob[(r.rob.factor=='unit')&(r.rob.method=='lotcp_s')&(r.rob.m==10)].lot.nunique())}} units) gives coverage {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} for LotCP-S at $m=10$ with a mean width of {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10,'width'):.2f}"}} % DMC, {{f"{r.robf('unit','scp_sample',0):.3f}"}} for sample-fold split conformal prediction and {{f"{r.robf('unit','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} for bias correction with the source quantile. The share of unit-draws with coverage below 0.80 is higher than at lot level ({{f"{100*r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10,'under'):.0f}"}} %), because single-instrument units differ more in residual scale than lots that average over instruments.

*Outlier screening.* Removing the scans flagged by the data provider from calibration and evaluation leaves coverage unchanged ({{f"{r.robcov('outliers_removed',1,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}) and narrows the intervals by {{r.pct(r.robcov('outliers_removed',1,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} %.

*Number of past lots.* With only 10 past lots the lot-balanced quantile is close to the largest calibration score and the intervals are very wide; with 20 lots coverage is {{f"{r.robcov('n_cal_lots',20,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and with 40 lots {{f"{r.robcov('n_cal_lots',40,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}, at widths {{r.pct(r.robcov('n_cal_lots',20,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % and {{r.pct(r.robcov('n_cal_lots',40,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % above those obtained with all lots. The method is always conservative when lots are few, which follows from the $1/(K_m+1)$ mass at infinity in Eq. (6), and some 40 past lots are needed before the conservatism becomes small.

*Tuning constants.* The number of episodes (5 to 100) and the pseudo-count of the scale estimate (5 to 80) change coverage by less than {{f"{max(abs(r.robcov('n_episodes',k,'lotcp_s',10)-r.m('lotcp_s',10)) for k in (5,10,100)) + 0.0005:.3f}"}} and {{f"{max(abs(r.robcov('nu0',k,'lotcp_s',10)-r.m('lotcp_s',10)) for k in (5,20,80)) + 0.0005:.3f}"}} respectively.

# 10. Computational analysis (RQ5)

The cost of the method is dominated by the season-held-out fits, one per source season, which are done once when the calibration is built (Table 11). Energy use was not measured; on the CPU used here the complete cross-fitting for one target season took {{f"{r.xfit('pls'):.0f}"}} s for PLS and {{f"{r.xfit('cnn')/60:.0f}"}} min for the CNN. # 11. Error analysis (RQ5)

![Fig. 9. Error analysis at m = 10 (PLS). (a) Lot coverage against the absolute lot bias. (b) Lot coverage against the ratio of the lot's residual standard deviation to the prior scale. (c) Coverage by reference DMC range.](figures/fig9_error_analysis.png){width=6.5in}

We examined coverage at the level of the lot and of the scan for $m=10$ (Fig. 9). Of the {{r.nlot_err()}} target lots, {{r.lotfail('scp_sample')}} have coverage below 0.80 under sample-fold split conformal prediction, {{r.lotfail('lotcp')}} under LotCP and {{r.lotfail('lotcp_s')}} under LotCP-S. For split conformal prediction, lot coverage is governed by the absolute lot bias (Spearman correlation {{r.rho('scp_sample','abs_bias')}}). After bias correction the bias no longer matters much, and the remaining failures are lots whose residual spread exceeds what past lots suggest: the correlation between lot coverage and the ratio of the lot's residual standard deviation to the prior scale is {{r.rho('lotcp','ratio')}} for LotCP and {{r.rho('lotcp_s','ratio')}} for LotCP-S. The scale estimate of LotCP-S reduces the number of badly covered lots but cannot identify a wide lot reliably from ten reference samples and the repeated scans; the lot with the lowest coverage has a residual spread of {{r.worst_lot_ratio()}} times the prior scale.

By subgroup, LotCP-S coverage is {{r.sub('physio_stage','green','lotcp_s')}} for fruit at the green stage and {{r.sub('physio_stage','ripen','lotcp_s')}} for fruit at the ripening stage; {{r.sub('temp','low','lotcp_s')}}, {{r.sub('temp','mid','lotcp_s')}} and {{r.sub('temp','high','lotcp_s')}} for the low, medium and high fruit-temperature classes; {{r.sub('inst_seen','True','lotcp_s')}} for instrument units present in the source seasons and {{r.sub('inst_seen','False','lotcp_s')}} for units that are new in the target season; and {{r.sub('cult_seen','True','lotcp_s')}} and {{r.sub('cult_seen','False','lotcp_s')}} for cultivars present in and absent from the source seasons. The lower coverage at the ripening stage is in line with the finding of the data set's authors that physiological stage affects model performance more than cultivar, season or region [@anderson2020]. Among cultivars the lowest coverage is {{r.sub('cultivar','keitt','lotcp_s')}} for Keitt. Scans carrying the provider's first outlier flag are covered at {{r.sub('outlier_flag','1','lotcp_s')}} ({{r.sub('outlier_flag','1','scp_sample')}} under sample-fold split conformal prediction). Coverage is lower at the extremes of the DMC range (Fig. 9c), a known limitation of scores that do not adapt to the predicted value. These figures are a reminder that the guarantee is marginal: a lot of ripening fruit, or a lot measured with atypical scatter, can be covered below the nominal level even when coverage is nominal on average.

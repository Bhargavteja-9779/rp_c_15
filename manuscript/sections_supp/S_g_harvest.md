# Supplementary Note S7. Full harvest-decision and planning analysis (exploratory)

This note keeps the complete decision analysis of the first submission draft. Its operating points were chosen after the results were known; the main text (Section 2) reports the analysis at an operating point fixed in advance with season-cluster confidence intervals, which supersedes the statements here where they differ.

This section asks what the calibrated bounds change in the decision they are meant to support. Table 106 and Fig. 14 give the outcomes of the six decision rules for a specification of 15 % DMC; the specifications of 14 and 16 % and the CNN predictor lead to the same conclusions (supplementary tables S16–S18).

## S7.1 Fruit-level decisions

Accepting a fruit when its predicted DMC reaches the specification, the decision that a handheld reading invites, accepts {{r.hvp('point',0,None,'compliant_accepted')}} % of the mature fruit but lets through {{r.hvp('point',0,None,digits=1)}} % immature fruit among those accepted, and in {{r.hvp('point',0,None,'lots_over_10pct')}} % of lots more than one accepted fruit in ten is immature. Correcting the lot bias with ten reference fruit lowers the immature share to {{r.hvp('point_bc',10,None,digits=1)}} %, but leaves {{r.hvp('point_bc',10,None,'lots_over_10pct')}} % of lots above the 10 % mark, because a decision on the point value ignores the scatter of individual fruit around it.

A lower bound at α = 0.10 changes the picture. Without reference fruit, the split-conformal bound reduces the immature share to {{r.hvp('scp_lower',0,0.1,digits=1)}} % while accepting {{r.hvp('scp_lower',0,0.1,'compliant_accepted')}} % of mature fruit. With ten reference fruit, the LotCP-S bound reduces it to {{r.hvp('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.1,digits=1)}} %, leaves {{r.hvp('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.1,'lots_over_10pct')}} % of lots above the 10 % mark, and accepts {{r.hvp('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.1,'compliant_accepted')}} % of mature fruit, more than the split-conformal bound although its risk is lower. The reference fruit remove the lot bias, so the bound can sit closer to the prediction for the same protection. LotCP-A at the same level is more cautious ({{r.hvp('lotcp_a_lower',10,0.1,digits=1)}} % immature among accepted, {{r.hvp('lotcp_a_lower',10,0.1,'compliant_accepted')}} % of mature fruit accepted), as its lot-level guarantee requires.

The curves in Fig. 14a make the comparison independent of the chosen level. Over its whole range, every bound that uses ten reference fruit lies below the curves of the bounds without reference fruit: for a similar share of mature fruit accepted, it lets through about half as many immature fruit or fewer. The curve of LotCP-S lies on top of that of bias correction with a source margin; at fruit level, once the bias is corrected and the level is free, the two make practically the same decisions. The bias-corrected point decision is simply the end of the same curve (α = 0.5), where most mature fruit are accepted and the immature share is highest. The value of the reference fruit for the harvest decision is therefore large and robust, while the additional value of LotCP-S over the simpler margin lies elsewhere: in calibration, which matters when the level is set from costs (Supplementary Note S7), and in the lot-level assurance.

## S7.2 Lot-level decisions

Whether a block is picked now is decided per lot. Of the {{V:n_target_lots}} target lots, {{int(r.lotd('point',0,None,'ready_lots'))}} were ready by the criterion of Section 2 (at least 80 % of fruit at or above 15 % DMC). Deciding on point predictions harvested {{r.lotdp('point',0,None)}} % of the {{int(r.lotd('point',0,None,'not_ready_lots'))}} lots that were not ready and delayed {{r.lotdp('point',0,None,'delayed')}} % of the ready ones (Table 106). With five to ten reference fruit and a calibrated bound at α = 1/3, premature harvests fall to {{r.lotdp('lotcp_s_lower',10,1/3)}}–{{r.lotdp('lotcp_s_lower',5,1/3)}} % at the same rate of delay ({{r.lotdp('lotcp_s_lower',10,1/3,'delayed')}}–{{r.lotdp('lotcp_s_lower',5,1/3,'delayed')}} %), and at α = 0.20 to {{r.lotdp('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.2)}} % with {{r.lotdp('lotcp_s_lower',10,0.2,'delayed')}} % of ready lots delayed (Fig. 14b). Without reference fruit, no conformal bound reaches this frontier: the split-conformal bound harvests {{r.lotdp('scp_lower',0,1/3)}} % of not-ready lots while delaying {{r.lotdp('scp_lower',0,1/3,'delayed')}} % of ready ones at α = 1/3. As at fruit level, LotCP-S and bias correction with a source margin trace nearly the same lot-level curve. At this post hoc operating point (α = 1/3) the reference fruit appeared to halve premature lot harvests; with season-cluster confidence intervals at the operating point of the main text (Section 4.8), the difference between rules with and without reference fruit is not resolved.

## S7.3 Expected cost under assumed cost ratios

Table 107 and Fig. 15 weigh the two errors and the reference tests under the cost assumptions of Section 3. Harvesting every fruit costs {{f"{100*r.econ('accept_all',0,0.1):.0f}"}} units per 100 fruit, because {{f"{100*r.econ('accept_all',0,0.1):.0f}"}} % of the fruit is below 15 % DMC. Deciding on point predictions costs {{f"{100*r.econ('point',0,0.05):.1f}"}}–{{f"{100*r.econ('point',0,1/3):.1f}"}} units across the four cost ratios. A LotCP-S lower bound at the cost-derived level α\* with five reference fruit costs {{f"{100*r.econ('lotcp_s_lower',5,0.05):.1f}"}}–{{f"{100*r.econ('lotcp_s_lower',5,1/3):.1f}"}} units, including the cost of the reference tests; relative to point decisions this is a reduction of {{f"{100*min(r.econ_saving(a)[1] for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3)):.0f}"}}–{{f"{100*max(r.econ_saving(a)[1] for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3)):.0f}"}} % at the 15 % specification and {{f"{100*min(min(r.econ_saving(a)) for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3)):.0f}"}}–{{f"{100*max(max(r.econ_saving(a)) for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3)):.0f}"}} % across the three specifications. Most of this reduction comes from using a bound instead of a point value: without any reference fruit, the split-conformal bound at α\* already costs {{f"{100*r.econ('scp_lower',0,0.05):.1f}"}}–{{f"{100*r.econ('scp_lower',0,1/3):.1f}"}} units. In this post hoc sweep the reference fruit appeared to add a small saving when withholding a mature fruit is relatively expensive; at the fixed operating point of the main text, with season-cluster intervals, no saving from reference fruit was detectable (Section 4.8).

The cost-derived level works for LotCP-S because its bound is calibrated. Using α\* instead of the best level on the grid chosen in hindsight raises the cost of LotCP-S with ten reference fruit by {{f"{100*r.regret('pls','lotcp_s_lower',10)[0]:.1f}"}} % on average (at most {{f"{100*r.regret('pls','lotcp_s_lower',10)[1]:.0f}"}} %) with PLS and by {{f"{100*r.regret('cnn','lotcp_s_lower',10)[0]:.1f}"}} % with the CNN (supplementary table S17b). The two simpler bounds are well tuned for one predictor and not for the other: for bias correction with a source margin the average regret is {{f"{100*r.regret('pls','bc_lower',10)[0]:.1f}"}} % with PLS, and for the split-conformal bound it is {{f"{100*r.regret('cnn','scp_lower',0)[0]:.1f}"}} % with the CNN. In other words, with LotCP-S a grower can translate a cost ratio into a confidence level and expect the decision to be close to the best one available, whichever predictor the instrument uses.

*How many reference fruit?* Each reference test costs one and a half fruit, so its value depends on how many fruit share it. For lots of 1000 fruit the cost-minimising number is {{min(r.econ_best_m(a,1000) for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3))}}–{{max(r.econ_best_m(a,1000) for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3))}} across the cost ratios, for lots of 5000 fruit {{min(r.econ_best_m(a,5000) for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3))}}–{{max(r.econ_best_m(a,5000) for a in (0.05,0.1,0.2,1/3))}}, and for lots of 200 fruit it is {{r.econ_best_m(0.1,200)}} at c_D = 1/9: on small lots under these assumptions destructive testing does not pay for itself and the bound without reference fruit is preferable (Table 107, Fig. 15b). These optima rest on the assumed costs and on the retrospective simulation; they illustrate how the planner and the cost model combine, and a grower would substitute local values.


## S7.4 Planning the reference budget

Fig. 13 compares the width planned from the source seasons (Section 2) with the width realised on the lots of the target season, for the five target seasons, three predictors, five values of $m$ and both variants.

![Fig. 13. Reference-budget planning. (a) Interval width planned from past lots against the mean width realised on the lots of the following season; each point is one target season, predictor, number of reference samples and variant. (b) Planned and realised budget curves for PLS, averaged over target seasons.](figures/fig13_planning.png){width=6.3in}

The planned width is a usable forecast. Over all {{len(r.plan)}} combinations the median absolute relative difference between realised and planned width is {{f"{100*r.plan.rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} %, and the mean difference is {{f"{100*r.plan.rel_error.mean():.0f}"}} %. For PLS and LightGBM the median absolute difference is {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.model=='pls'].rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} % and {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.model=='gbm'].rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} %; for the CNN it is {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.model=='cnn'].rel_error.abs().median():.0f}"}} %. By target season the mean difference ranges from {{f"{100*r.plan.groupby('target').rel_error.mean().min():.0f}"}} % to {{f"{100*r.plan.groupby('target').rel_error.mean().max():.0f}"}} %: the forecast is too wide for 2017, when the calibration rested on two source seasons whose season-held-out models were weak, and within {{f"{100*r.plan[r.plan.target>2017].groupby('target').rel_error.mean().abs().max():.0f}"}} % for the four later seasons (supplementary table S15). The shape of the curve is reproduced (Fig. 13b): most of the reduction in width is obtained with the first five reference samples, and beyond ten both curves flatten. The largest deviations in Fig. 13a are the CNN in 2017, where the planned width is well above the realised one. Table 108 puts the planner in the form a grower would use: for each number of reference fruit, the width of the error bar to expect, the share of lots in which it will hold for at least 90 % of the fruit, and the cost of the tests. Going from no reference fruit to three narrows the LotCP-S interval from {{f"{r.plannerv(0,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} to {{f"{r.plannerv(3,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} % DMC; going from ten to twenty narrows it only from {{f"{r.plannerv(10,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} to {{f"{r.plannerv(20,'planned_width_lotcp_s'):.1f}"}} % DMC, at twice the laboratory effort. A grower who needs the error bar to hold within nine lots in ten chooses LotCP-A and accepts a wider interval ({{f"{r.plannerv(10,'planned_width_lotcp_a'):.1f}"}} % DMC at m = 10). Combined with the cost model of Supplementary Note S7, the planner turns the question "how many fruit should we cut?" into a calculation that can be made before the season from the calibration set.

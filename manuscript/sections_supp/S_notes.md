# Supplementary Note S1. Robustness and sensitivity

Fig. S2 and supplementary table S22 summarise how the coverage of LotCP-S responds to errors in the reference values, to outlier screening, to the number of past lots and to other source–target shifts.


![Fig. S2. Robustness of LotCP-S coverage (PLS, nominal 0.90): (a) Gaussian error added to the reference values of the m reference samples; (b) number of past lots available for calibration; (c) other source–target shifts.](figures/fig8_robustness.png){width=6.5in}

*Error in the reference values.* Oven-drying has its own error, and the $m$ reference values carry it. Adding Gaussian error with a standard deviation of 0.25 or 0.5 % DMC to the reference samples at deployment (not to the calibration lots) leaves coverage at or slightly above nominal ({{f"{r.robcov('label_noise_sd',0.5,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$ for 0.5 % DMC) and widens the intervals by {{r.pct(r.robcov('label_noise_sd',0.5,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % (supplementary table S22). The noisier references inflate the estimated lot scale, which errs on the conservative side.

*Reuse of the calibration lots.* The variance components and the pseudo-count are estimated from the same past lots that provide the conformal quantile. Estimating them on a random half of the lots and computing the quantile on the other half, with the pseudo-count fixed at 10, gives coverage {{f"{r.robf('split_constants','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and an interval score of {{f"{r.robf('split_constants','lotcp_s',10,'iscore'):.2f}"}} at $m=10$, against {{r.c('lotcp_s',10)}} and {{r.i('lotcp_s',10)}}; the reuse does not make the intervals optimistic.

*Single-instrument units.* In the data set a lot was usually scanned with several instrument units, whereas a grower would use one. Redefining the deployment unit as one instrument unit within a lot (reference and query scans from that unit only; {{int(r.rob[(r.rob.factor=='unit')&(r.rob.method=='lotcp_s')&(r.rob.m==10)].lot.nunique())}} units) gives coverage {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} for LotCP-S at $m=10$ with a mean width of {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10,'width'):.2f}"}} % DMC, {{f"{r.robf('unit','scp_sample',0):.3f}"}} for sample-fold split conformal prediction and {{f"{r.robf('unit','biascorr_naive',10):.3f}"}} for bias correction with the source quantile. The interval score of LotCP-S is {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10,'iscore'):.2f}"}}, against {{f"{r.robf('unit','target_only_centred',10,'iscore'):.2f}"}} for centred target-only calibration at $m=10$ and {{f"{r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',20,'iscore'):.2f}"}} against {{f"{r.robf('unit','target_only_centred',20,'iscore'):.2f}"}} at $m=20$. The share of unit-draws with coverage below 0.80 is higher than at lot level ({{f"{100*r.robf('unit','lotcp_s',10,'under'):.0f}"}} %), because single-instrument units differ more in residual scale than lots that average over instruments.

*Outlier screening.* Removing the scans flagged by the data provider from calibration and evaluation leaves coverage unchanged ({{f"{r.robcov('outliers_removed',1,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}) and narrows the intervals by {{r.pct(r.robcov('outliers_removed',1,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} %.

*Number of past lots.* With only 10 past lots the lot-balanced quantile is close to the largest calibration score and the intervals are very wide; with 20 lots coverage is {{f"{r.robcov('n_cal_lots',20,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and with 40 lots {{f"{r.robcov('n_cal_lots',40,'lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}, at widths {{r.pct(r.robcov('n_cal_lots',20,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % and {{r.pct(r.robcov('n_cal_lots',40,'lotcp_s',10,'width'), r.m('lotcp_s',10,'width'))}} % above those obtained with all lots. The method is always conservative when lots are few, which follows from the $1/(K_m+1)$ mass at infinity in Eq. (6), and some 40 past lots are needed before the conservatism becomes small.

*Tuning constants.* The number of episodes (5 to 100) and the pseudo-count of the scale estimate (5 to 80) change coverage by less than {{f"{max(abs(r.robcov('n_episodes',k,'lotcp_s',10)-r.m('lotcp_s',10)) for k in (5,10,100)) + 0.0005:.3f}"}} and {{f"{max(abs(r.robcov('nu0',k,'lotcp_s',10)-r.m('lotcp_s',10)) for k in (5,20,80)) + 0.0005:.3f}"}} respectively.

*Nominal level.* Supplementary table S23 gives the results for nominal levels 0.80 and 0.95. The pattern is the same: sample-fold split conformal prediction under-covers and LotCP-S with $m\ge5$ covers slightly above the nominal level.


*Other shifts.* When only the two seasons before the target are used as source, LotCP-S still covers {{f"{r.robcov('protocol','recent2','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} at $m=10$, with wider intervals ({{f"{r.robcov('protocol','recent2','lotcp_s',10,'width'):.2f}"}} % DMC) because fewer and less diverse past lots are available. When a whole growing region or a whole cultivar is held out, coverage at $m=10$ is {{f"{r.robcov('protocol','region','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}} and {{f"{r.robcov('protocol','cultivar','lotcp_s',10):.3f}"}}. In these two protocols the source contains all seasons, so they test a region or cultivar shift without a season shift.

# Supplementary Note S2. Computational analysis


The cost of the method is dominated by the season-held-out fits, one per source season, which are done once when the calibration is built (supplementary table S24). Energy use was not measured; on the CPU used here the complete cross-fitting for one target season took {{f"{r.xfit('pls'):.0f}"}} s for PLS and {{f"{r.xfit('cnn')/60:.0f}"}} min for the CNN. The episodic calibration itself takes about {{f"{r.cost['lotcp_s_m10']['calibration_seconds']:.1f}"}} s for {{r.cost['lotcp_s_m10']['n_cal_lots']}} past lots and 30 episodes, and the deployment step is a bias and a scale per lot, which is negligible on the embedded processor of a handheld instrument. The relevant cost is the number of destructive reference measurements. Fig. 4 expresses the trade-off: with LotCP-S and PLS, three reference samples per lot give a mean width of {{r.w('lotcp_s',3)}} % DMC, ten give {{r.w('lotcp_s',10)}}, and thirty give {{r.w('lotcp_s',30)}}; most of the gain is obtained with the first five to ten samples.

# Supplementary Note S3. Error analysis

![Fig. S3. Error analysis at m = 10 (PLS). (a) Lot coverage against the absolute lot bias. (b) Lot coverage against the ratio of the lot's residual standard deviation to the prior scale. (c) Coverage by reference DMC range.](figures/fig9_error_analysis.png){width=6.5in}

We examined coverage at the level of the lot and of the scan for $m=10$ (Fig. S3). Of the {{r.nlot_err()}} target lots, {{r.lotfail('scp_sample')}} have coverage below 0.80 under sample-fold split conformal prediction, {{r.lotfail('lotcp')}} under LotCP and {{r.lotfail('lotcp_s')}} under LotCP-S. For split conformal prediction, lot coverage is governed by the absolute lot bias (Spearman correlation {{r.rho('scp_sample','abs_bias')}}). After bias correction the bias no longer matters much, and the remaining failures are lots whose residual spread exceeds what past lots suggest: the correlation between lot coverage and the ratio of the lot's residual standard deviation to the prior scale is {{r.rho('lotcp','ratio')}} for LotCP and {{r.rho('lotcp_s','ratio')}} for LotCP-S. The scale estimate of LotCP-S reduces the number of badly covered lots but cannot identify a wide lot reliably from ten reference samples and the repeated scans; the lot with the lowest coverage has a residual spread of {{r.worst_lot_ratio()}} times the prior scale.

By subgroup, LotCP-S coverage is {{r.sub('physio_stage','green','lotcp_s')}} for fruit at the green stage and {{r.sub('physio_stage','ripen','lotcp_s')}} for fruit at the ripening stage; {{r.sub('temp','low','lotcp_s')}}, {{r.sub('temp','mid','lotcp_s')}} and {{r.sub('temp','high','lotcp_s')}} for the low, medium and high fruit-temperature classes; {{r.sub('inst_seen','True','lotcp_s')}} for instrument units present in the source seasons and {{r.sub('inst_seen','False','lotcp_s')}} for units that are new in the target season; and {{r.sub('cult_seen','True','lotcp_s')}} and {{r.sub('cult_seen','False','lotcp_s')}} for cultivars present in and absent from the source seasons. The lower coverage at the ripening stage is in line with the finding of the data set's authors that physiological stage affects model performance more than cultivar, season or region [@anderson2020]. Among cultivars the lowest coverage is {{r.sub('cultivar','keitt','lotcp_s')}} for Keitt. Scans carrying the provider's first outlier flag are covered at {{r.sub('outlier_flag','1','lotcp_s')}} ({{r.sub('outlier_flag','1','scp_sample')}} under sample-fold split conformal prediction). Coverage is lower at the extremes of the DMC range (Fig. S3c), a known limitation of scores that do not adapt to the predicted value. These figures are a reminder that the guarantee is marginal: a lot of ripening fruit, or a lot measured with atypical scatter, can be covered below the nominal level even when coverage is nominal on average.
